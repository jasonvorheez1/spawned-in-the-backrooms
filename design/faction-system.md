# Faction design

Players earn fame and infamy separately with each organized faction. Their visible standing reflects both, while karma remains independent. A good deed for one group does not automatically harm another; opposing outcomes should change standing only when the story makes the consequence clear. Existing saved net reputation remains readable.

People respond to their own faction and witnessed incidents. A bump or a drawn weapon alone is not an assault. A warning precedes retaliation for an accidental first hit; persistent faction hostility should require serious infamy, with a reason the player can inspect. Neutral camps should not become hostile because another faction dislikes the player.

Uniforms can conceal reputation from a matching faction at a distance. Recognition nearby and a witnessed offense can still expose the player. Clothing must not change permanent reputation, erase personal incidents, or grant quest rewards.

Faction jobs should offer clear terms and optional approaches, report actual rewards and change standing only for relevant actions. Characters and locations need meaningful alternate paths when a faction will not cooperate. Track individual factions rather than treating all traders or survivors as allies.

Further work: give the journal a history of specific reputation changes and witnesses; add authored reputation-gated conversations and nonviolent ways to rebuild standing; audit quest outcomes, faction membership, disguises, and rewards in each level. Playtest hostility and mixed reputations in the live game before changing thresholds.
